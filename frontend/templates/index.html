Thesis Monograph & Architecture Specification

Forecasting Air Quality Using Time-Series Analysis at Jali Station

A Study of PM2.5 Concentration Trends and Predictive Modeling

1. Abstract & System Context

Air pollution, particularly fine particulate matter (PM2.5), remains an important environmental and public-health concern in rapidly urbanising cities. This study investigated temporal patterns and short-term forecasting of daily PM2.5 concentration at the Jali monitoring station in Kigali, Rwanda.

The analysis used 8,274 hourly REMA air-quality observations covering 5 March 2022 to 29 February 2024 and 94,672 ten-minute Meteo Rwanda observations. A single, consistent preprocessing pipeline was applied to the PM2.5 series: timestamps were ordered and de-duplicated, extreme observations were capped using the 1.5×IQR rule, short gaps were linearly interpolated, remaining gaps were treated using hour-of-day means, and the resulting series was aggregated to daily means.

2. Predictive Modeling & Evaluation

Six primary forecasting approaches were evaluated on the same chronological 72-day held-out test period: Seasonal Naive, ARIMA, SARIMA, Random Forest, XGBoost, and Prophet. ARIMA and SARIMA orders were selected using AIC-guided searches, while Random Forest and XGBoost used lag, rolling, and calendar features designed to avoid leakage.

Model performance was evaluated using RMSE, MAE, and MAPE. Random Forest produced the lowest test-set RMSE (5.93 µg/m³), MAE (4.42 µg/m³), and MAPE (22.08%), narrowly outperforming XGBoost. It was refitted on the full daily series for a native seven-day forecast ranging from 15.30 to 16.51 µg/m³.

3. Meteorological Context & SARIMAX

Meteorological analysis was treated separately because only 206 daily observations overlapped between the REMA and Meteo Rwanda records. Pearson associations were generally weak; radiation had the largest absolute correlation (r = −0.2511). In the supplementary SARIMAX association analysis, humidity, lagged temperature, and lagged humidity were statistically significant at the 5% level.
